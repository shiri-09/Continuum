# Continuum: literature review and evidence register

Research date: 26 September 2026. Companion to `CONTINUUM_MASTER_SPEC.md`.

## Scope and method

This is a targeted, traceable literature and product review for a nine-hour prototype, not a preregistered systematic review or exhaustive claim to have read every AAC paper. Searches covered dysarthria, ALS/MND, cerebral palsy, stroke, alternative access, multimodal scanning, communication-device provision, personalized speech recognition, LLM-assisted AAC and current product documentation. Primary journal papers, author-hosted manuscripts, professional clinical guidance and official product sources were prioritized. Reviews are labelled as reviews; company documentation is not independent efficacy evidence. Abstract-only findings are kept within what their abstracts establish.

The central distinction is between **communication need**, **motor access**, **language comprehension**, **device provision**, **actual use**, and **participation**. They are different constructs. A speech impairment percentage cannot be used as the percentage requiring this application. A procurement failure cannot be interpreted as a percentage of an entire diagnostic population. Cohort findings cannot be extrapolated to India or the world without representative data and a justified model.

## Clinical and access foundations

The prototype targets a usable ability profile rather than a diagnosis: difficulty relying on ordinary speech and hand input, ability to use its offered choices, and an intentional movement detectable with the available hardware. The implemented camera choices are mouth or eyebrow movement. Users with eye movement alone would need a different implemented and evaluated detector; naming locked-in syndrome does not supply that capability.

Dysarthria concerns speech motor execution. Apraxia concerns speech-movement planning; aphasia concerns language. They can coexist. A person with severe dysarthria may understand language well, but that cannot be assumed for every person with a neurological diagnosis. There must be no software inference that lack of movement means disagreement, reduced understanding, lack of consciousness or consent. Sources: [ASHA dysarthria](https://www.asha.org/Practice-Portal/Clinical-Topics/Dysarthria-in-Adults/), [NIDCD apraxia](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/apraxia-speech), [NIDCD aphasia](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/aphasia).

ASHA describes both direct access and scanning using an agreed movement, vocalization or switch. Device selection considers physical access, vision, language, environment and communication partners. Its guidance does not prescribe a universal gesture or require everyone to use an advanced electronic device. [ASHA AAC practice portal](https://www.asha.org/Practice-Portal/Professional-Issues/Augmentative-and-Alternative-Communication/)

Prices and whole-market rankings are excluded because these depend on geography, hardware, subscription and support arrangements. ## Research opportunities and evaluation design

The best near-term experiment is **draft-preserving recovery under input interruption**, because its conditions can be controlled and its outcome is observable. The practical question is whether an interrupted message can be completed without unwanted output and without recreating the draft. Compare identical vocabularies, scan speeds and tasks; change only the recovery policy. Include interrupted and uninterrupted trials in counterbalanced order.

For camera validation, separate enrollment from later trials and include ordinary movement, looking away, pose change, lighting change, no face, stale frames, occlusion during a gesture and prolonged gestures. Human labels are needed to distinguish intended from unintended actions. Report missed gestures alongside false selections per idle minute, and nuisance pauses alongside recovery time. Face-model scores are not probabilities of intention. Do not infer fatigue, distress or diagnosis from them.

For language personalization, compare the shipped ranker with static ordering and a frequency-only method. Use temporally later held-out requests and report selection steps, time, edits and correction effort. The existing synthetic test shows that counts affect later ranking; it says nothing about clinical benefit or novel-message prediction. Keep suggestions stable while scanning and make each final message user-approved. No method should silently replace the person's intended wording.

For future user research, co-design task selection and outcomes, compensate participants, ensure accessible consent, offer breaks, and keep existing communication tools available. A study may find that a physical switch or an established AAC product is better for a participant. That is a useful finding, not an experiment failure. Do not use emergency calls or treatment decisions as prototype test tasks.

Proposed societal outcomes include greater ability to initiate topics, refuse unwanted actions, repair misunderstandings, maintain relationships and choose personal vocabulary. These should be measured directly through user-defined goals and sustained use. Lower latency or higher gesture accuracy alone cannot establish those outcomes.

## Honest claims and remaining questions

- Implemented: locally trained count-based phrase/context ranking and smoothed word bigrams; no cloud inference in that module. It is not our own general LLM.
- Implemented access direction: personally calibrated mouth or eyebrow gestures over pretrained facial features, plus switch input. It is not a universal interface for locked-in syndrome.
- Established prior art: scanning, alternative access, personalization, multimodal access and AI suggestions.
- Proposed differentiator: transparent authorization and recovery under interruption, backed by reproducible tests and later user evaluation.
- Unresolved: real-world frequency of harmful access interruptions; whether extra confirmation helps more than it burdens; whether target users find setup tolerable; comparative behavior against current commercial software; Indian-language quality and service pathways.
- Not established: clinical efficacy, universal accessibility, reduced caregiver burden, improved quality of life, prevention of medical errors, or a global addressable population size.

The strongest presentation is therefore a concrete communication task, an honest statement of existing work, a clearly isolated experiment and its actual results. The next scientific step is user involvement, not a larger unsupported novelty claim.

## Implementation addendum — local generative tool proposals

The optional pretrained Qwen3 assistant is now implemented separately from the locally count-trained phrase ranker. A live developer demonstration completed request → local model proposal → explicit approval → note creation → on-disk content verification. This is engineering evidence, not a new published result. The current bounded tool set covers local notes, two application launch requests and approved HTTPS destinations. External windows may require assisted return; general desktop control has not been demonstrated. The existing SpeakFaster, ACAT, Gameface and commercial AAC comparisons remain applicable; adding an LLM or a confirmation step does not establish novelty or product superiority.

The additional research opportunity is **accessible review of model-proposed actions**. Evaluate whether the user can understand the exact tool/arguments, reject semantic errors and recover from input loss before approval. Measure appropriate rejection and missed intentions together; a system that refuses everything is not useful. Models have produced incorrect or unsupported candidates in development. Syntactic validation, local inference and explicit confirmation do not establish semantic reliability or clinical safety. See section 27 of the master specification and the updated presentation brief for the implemented scope and observed demonstration.
